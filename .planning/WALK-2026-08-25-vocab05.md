# Operator walk — VOCAB-05 + the John Tsatsimas write

**Where:** Claude Desktop, with the Lightning Visuals operator plugin.
**Plugin version required:** 0.17.0. Already installed and verified by content, not by
version string (`arms this send and nothing else` is present in the installed
`enrich-records` skill).
**Time:** 20–30 minutes.
**Money:** roughly $0.10–0.20 and a handful of provider credits, all shown to you before
anything is spent. Nothing here can spend without you saying yes first.

---

## The one rule for this walk

**Say everything in your own words.** Do not copy the example lines below literally unless
that happens to be how you'd say it anyway. They are there to tell you *what to ask for*,
not *how to phrase it*.

If at any point you are told to type a particular string, or a plain "yes" is not accepted,
**that is the defect** — write it down and keep going. That is the whole point of this walk.

Keep a note as you go. For each step, one line: what you said, what happened, and whether
anything felt like it was speaking a computer's language at you.

---

## Part A — see where things stand (2 min, spends nothing)

**A1.** Start a fresh conversation. Ask what the backend is doing.

> *e.g.* "What's the enrichment backend doing right now?"

**Expect:** a plain-language status — which workflows are on, whether anything is running,
anything stuck, how many records are waiting on a human.

**Check:** it should tell you, unprompted, that live writes are **off**. It should not ask
you to type anything to find that out.

**A2.** Ask whether you can write to HubSpot at all right now.

> *e.g.* "Can you write to HubSpot at the moment?"

**Expect:** yes-in-principle, with the honest caveat that each send needs your go-ahead.

## Part B — the acceptance test: John Tsatsimas (10 min)

This is the record the last walk got stuck on. It is currently **enriched but never
written** — the data was derived and then blocked at the write. This walk finishes it.

**B1.** Ask for the update, naming the person and the company. Nothing else.

> *e.g.* "Update John Tsatsimas from Football NSW"

**Expect:** it finds the contact (HubSpot id `347569451461`) by name, or asks you a
clarifying question a normal person would ask ("which Football NSW record?"). It should

**B2.** Let it show you the preview and the cost.

**Expect:** how many records, which providers will be called, the estimated spend, and what

## Part C — the grant path (10 min)

A grant is the "don't ask me every time" mode. It is opened once and covers a session.

**C1.** Ask for standing permission, in your words.

> *e.g.* "Let me approve this once for the session instead of per send" or
> "Turn on live writes for this batch of work"

**Expect:** a proposal you can read before agreeing — which lane, which records, whether
creating new records is included, the **worst-case cost**, and what you are giving up by
not being asked each time. Nothing is open yet.

**Check:** it should tell you plainly that the worst case shown is a **disclosure, not a
limit** — it says what could be spent, it does not stop the spend.

**C2.** Agree, in your words.

**Expect:** the grant opens. It should name what it covers.

**Check:** it should not ask you for a phrase here either.

**C3.** Send something under the grant — a second enrichment, small.

> *e.g.* "Now enrich <another company you don't mind spending a credit on>"

**Expect:** the preview is still shown, but you are **not asked twice**. It should say
which grant the send is running under.

**Check:** the preview still appears. A grant removes the question, not the preview.

**C4.** Revoke it mid-flight if you can, otherwise just revoke it.

> *e.g.* "Stop that" / "Cancel the permission"

**Expect:** it tells you honestly that revoking **refuses the next send** and does **not**
stop a send already running.

**THE OPEN QUESTION THIS WALK MUST ANSWER:** sends go out in chunks of 2 records. A
40-record send is 20 chunks, and if you revoke after chunk 1, the other 19 still go. Is
that acceptable to you, or does revocation need to bite mid-run?

**Write down your answer.** It decides whether a whole extra phase gets built.

**C5.** Close the session cleanly.

> *e.g.* "We're done — turn writes off"

**Expect:** confirmation that writes are off and the backend is disarmed.

## Part D — the safety check (3 min, spends nothing)

This proves a casual yes cannot become a write. **Do this in a fresh conversation.**

**D1.** Say "yes" out of nowhere, before asking for anything.

> Type just: `yes`

**Expect:** confusion, or a question about what you're agreeing to. **Nothing is sent.**

**Fails if:** anything at all is dispatched. That is a serious defect — stop the walk and
say so.

**D2.** Ask a question, then answer a *different* one.

> *e.g.* ask "how many records are waiting on review?", and when it answers, say "yes, send
> them"

**Expect:** it asks you what you want sent and shows you the cost first. It must not treat
your yes as authorizing a send it never described.

## Part E — the review lane (optional, 5 min)

**This lane's new wording ships unwitnessed** — the changes above also removed a phrase
here, and Part B does not exercise it. Worth 5 minutes if you have them.

**E1.** Ask what needs review.

> *e.g.* "What's waiting for me to decide on?"

**E2.** Ask it to walk you through one record.

**Expect:** the conflict in plain language, what each source claims, and the **exact write**
approval would send.

**E3.** Approve or reject that one record, in your words.

**Expect:** your yes to that exact write is enough — no phrase. And it should be clear the
yes covers **that one record only**, not the queue.

**Note:** this lane also needs an administrator-set switch (`ALLOW_REVIEW_SUBMIT`). If it
tells you the write is blocked by that, that is expected and not a walk failure — record it
and move on.
