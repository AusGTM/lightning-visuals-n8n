<objective>
Widen what counts as identity so a person with a LinkedIn URL or a mobile number and no email is
sendable (D-16b), and stop the email-domain gate from holding a row that has no email to judge
(D-16c). Jen Ramamurthy from the 2026-09-18 pickleball round — mobile plus a LinkedIn profile, no
email — is the case that motivated it.

Purpose: provider discovery will return people the waterfall cannot always give an email for.
Without this, every one of them is held `no_email` before the discovery lane has proved anything.

Output: five identity groups in all three copies, a relaxed `hold_emailless`, a
`partition_for_dispatch` that skips the domain relation when there is no email, and regenerated
workflow JSON carrying the updated inlined `columnMap.js`.
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
**Noun:** the identity key that resolves a contact.
**Signal:** `optional` — `email` stops being the sole ingest identity anchor; and `pluralization`
— a second and third identity group join the existing three.
**Decision:** `add-alongside` — `email` stays the FIRST rung of an ordered ladder and the other
keys are added below it, rather than identity being promoted to a flat set of equals. Rationale:
D-16a's ladder is explicitly ordered (email, then linkedin_url, then mobilephone, then other) and
D-16a-i makes `phone` create-only, never a match rung — a flat set would lose both orderings.
**Accepted debt:** identity is now expressed in TWO shapes — an unordered `any_of` set in
`column_mapping.yaml` (what makes a row admissible) and an ordered ladder in the ingest lane's
match chain (what resolves it). These answer different questions and are deliberately not
unified; the ordering is expressed only in `matchProposal.js::laneOf` (plan 06).
**What would force a promote:** a fourth identity key whose ADMISSIBILITY also depends on its
position in the ladder. Nothing in this phase does.
</assumption_delta_decision>
